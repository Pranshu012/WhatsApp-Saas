PART 0 - WHAT YOU ARE BUILDING (read once, 10 minutes)
================================================================================

THE PRODUCT

  A web application that Indian small businesses subscribe to for
  Rs 1,999 per month. It automatically replies to their customers'
  WhatsApp messages.

  Example: a clothing shop. A customer messages "what are your timings?"
  on WhatsApp. Your software replies instantly with the shop's hours.
  The shop owner did nothing.

THE MONEY MODEL - THIS IS THE MOST IMPORTANT THING TO UNDERSTAND

  There are TWO separate bills:

    Bill 1:  Your customer pays YOU        Rs 1,999/month  (software)
    Bill 2:  Your customer pays META       per message     (WhatsApp charges)

  You NEVER pay for their messages. Meta bills them directly. This is
  called the "Tech Provider" model.

  Why this matters: it means you can never lose money on message volume.
  A customer sending 100,000 messages costs you nothing extra.

  It also means: if a bug in your code sends duplicate messages, you are
  spending YOUR CUSTOMER'S money. That is worse than spending your own.
  This is why the build plan is so careful about duplicate sends.

  Meta's India message rates (from 1 July 2026, before 18% GST):
      Marketing message        Rs 0.8631
      Utility message         Rs 0.1150
      Authentication          Rs 0.1150
      Service message         Rs 0.0000  -> Rs 0.1150 from 1 Oct 2026
      Incoming messages       FREE always

  Note the 1 October 2026 change. Service messages become billable.
  Your customers will get a bigger Meta bill and will ask you why.
  Your dashboard (built in step F20) is your answer.

WHY THE PRICE IS Rs 1,999 AND NOT Rs 2,000

  UPI payments under Rs 2,000 have ZERO transaction fee in India
  (NPCI waiver). Above Rs 2,000 you pay about 2.36% to Razorpay.

  On Rs 40,000 monthly revenue that is Rs 960/month saved. Ten times
  your entire server bill. Do not raise the price above Rs 1,999 without
  reading 12-SCALING/REVENUE-FUNDED-INFRASTRUCTURE.md first.

WHAT IT COSTS YOU TO RUN

  DURING THE PILOT
    Servers and services    about Rs 100/month  (almost all free tiers)
    Domain name             about Rs 1,000/year
    Accountant              Rs 0 - deferred
    TOTAL                   about Rs 100/month

  AFTER YOU REGISTER THE BUSINESS
    Servers and services    about Rs 100/month
    Accountant (GST)        Rs 1,000-2,500/month <- BIGGER THAN SERVERS
    Domain                  about Rs 1,000/year

  Yes, your accountant will cost 20 times your servers. That is normal
  for an Indian SaaS. It is also the single biggest reason to run the
  pilot first - you defer it until you know the product works.

  DURING THE PILOT YOU CHARGE Rs 500/month, collected by UPI manually.
  Not because Rs 5,000/month matters, but because a free user gives you
  polite feedback and a paying user gives you honest feedback. Someone
  who pays Rs 500 will consider Rs 1,999. Someone who won't pay Rs 500
  definitely won't pay Rs 1,999.

THE TECHNOLOGY (you do not need to understand this yet)

  Backend    Java 21 + Spring Boot (you already know this)
  Database   PostgreSQL 17
  Frontend   React
  Server     One free Oracle Cloud virtual machine
  Queue      PostgreSQL (NOT Redis, NOT Kafka - explained in 13-DECISIONS)

  You will NOT use: Redis, Kafka, Kubernetes, microservices, or AI.
  This is deliberate. Read 13-DECISIONS/ later to understand why.

The loop you will repeat 23 times:

    2. Paste the increment prompt from the PROMPTS folder
    3. READ THE PLAN it gives you. Correct it if wrong.

    8. Commit YOURSELF (Claude Code is not allowed to)

THE FOUR RULES THAT MATTER MOST:

  ONE INCREMENT PER SESSION.
      Two in one session is where quality drops. The context gets
      cluttered and it starts contradicting decisions from earlier.

  ALWAYS READ THE PLAN BEFORE APPROVING.
      Correcting a plan costs you one message. Correcting 600 lines of
      wrong code costs you an hour. Look specifically for: a new
      dependency you did not approve, a missing tenant_id, a WhatsApp
      API call in the wrong place.

  YOU RUN THE BUILD. YOU COMMIT.
      Do not accept "all tests pass" on trust. Run ./mvnw clean verify
      in the built-in terminal. If you cannot explain what a file does,
      ask before committing.

  ONE SESSION AT A TIME.
      The desktop app can run parallel sessions. Do not use them here.
      The increments depend on each other and two sessions will produce

THE TEN THINGS THAT WILL COST YOU THE MOST IF YOU GET THEM WRONG
================================================================================

 1. Registering a company, hiring a CA, and waiting for Meta verification
    BEFORE anyone has confirmed they want this. That is Rs 20,000+ and
    5 weeks spent on looking legitimate instead of on learning.
    Run the pilot first.

 2. Switching your Meta app to LIVE mode during the pilot. That is what
    triggers the Advanced Access requirement. Leave it in Development.

 3. Choosing the wrong Oracle Cloud region at signup. It cannot be
    changed. Mumbai or Hyderabad.

 4. Running tests as a PostgreSQL superuser. Superusers bypass row-level
    security, so your isolation tests pass while your isolation is
    broken. Testcontainers does this BY DEFAULT.

 5. Skipping multi-tenancy "because it's only a pilot". Adding tenant_id
    and RLS to a schema with live data is genuinely painful. Build it
    once, at the start, correctly.

 6. Not writing the ledger row BEFORE calling Meta's API. You end up with
    messages you cannot account for on your customer's invoice - and your
    customer pays that invoice, not you.

 7. Doing work inside the webhook handler. You must respond in under
    2 seconds. Verify, save, queue, return 200. Nothing else.

 8. Never testing a backup restore. Losing a pilot customer's data loses
    the customer AND the reference you needed to get the next fifty.

 9. Running a FREE pilot. Free users are polite and tell you nothing.
    Charge Rs 500 by UPI. The money is irrelevant; the signal is not.

10. Building Phase C features without doing the 15-20 customer
    conversations first. That is five weeks of work based on a guess.

BONUS, and it will bite you: not warning customers about Meta's separate
bill, loudly and repeatedly. It is the number one cause of angry
month-two conversations, in the pilot and after.
