# Value Proposition

## One sentence

> Answer every WhatsApp enquiry instantly — even at midnight — on your own WhatsApp number, for
> ₹1,999 a month.

## For the customer

| Their problem | What we do |
|---|---|
| Answers the same 20 questions daily | Automated FAQ and keyword replies |
| Enquiries after hours go cold | Instant replies, 24/7 |
| Forgets appointment reminders | Scheduled utility templates |
| No record beyond one phone | Shared inbox, full history |
| Can't grow past one person's typing speed | Automation scales; they don't |
| Doesn't understand WhatsApp charges | Message counts by category, visible |

## What makes us different

**1. Transparent pricing.** Flat ₹1,999 for software. Meta's charges go directly to the customer
at Meta's rates. No markup. Most competitors are BSPs adding 10–30% per message inside a single
bill you can't audit.

**2. The customer owns their WhatsApp account.** Their WABA, in their Meta portfolio, with their
payment method. They can disconnect us and keep the number, the history, and the templates.
That's not just fairness — it's the strongest trust argument available in a market where lock-in
is the norm.

**3. One vertical, properly.** Not a generic inbox for every business. The templates, automations,
and language fit their specific trade.

**4. Founder-level support.** For the first 20 customers, they get you on a call. Nobody funded
can match that, and it's temporary — use it while you have it.

## What we do NOT claim

Be honest about this internally, or you'll oversell and churn:

- Not more features than Wati or AiSensy — they have funded teams
- Not a nicer UI
- Not more integrations
- Not AI-powered (deliberately — ADR-007)
- Not the cheapest possible — free tools exist; they're worse

Competing on features against funded competitors is a losing fight. Competing on transparency,
ownership, and vertical depth is winnable.

## The message hierarchy

Lead with the outcome, not the mechanism:

```text
1. "You'll never miss an enquiry again."          ← the outcome
2. "It replies automatically, on your number."     ← the mechanism
3. "₹1,999/month, and you pay Meta directly
    for messages — about ₹100 for your volume."    ← the price, with the two-bill model upfront
4. "Set up in under an hour, on a call with me."   ← removes the effort objection
5. "Your account stays yours. Cancel anytime."     ← removes the risk objection
```

Do not lead with architecture, uptime, or security. Nobody asks. It matters, and it's invisible.

## Proof points to collect from customer #1

You have none yet. These are what to gather, and they're worth more than any feature:

- [ ] "We answered X enquiries automatically last month"
- [ ] "No-shows dropped from X% to Y%"
- [ ] "First reply time went from 4 hours to 10 seconds"
- [ ] "The owner got their evenings back"
- [ ] A quote, with a name and a business, and permission to use it

One specific number from one real clinic beats any amount of copywriting.

## Positioning against alternatives

| Alternative | Why they'd switch |
|---|---|
| **Doing it manually** | Time. This is the main competitor, not other software. |
| **WhatsApp Business app alone** | No automation, no API, 256-contact broadcast cap |
| **Wati / AiSensy / Interakt** | We don't mark up messages; they own their WABA; we're focused on their trade |
| **Unofficial tools** (browser automation, QR-linked) | Real ban risk on their number. We're on the official API. |
| **A generic chatbot builder** | Doesn't understand WhatsApp templates, categories, or windows |

**The honest read on unofficial tools:** they're cheaper and have no per-message fee. Some
customers will choose them. Your argument is that their number is their business, and Meta bans
numbers using unofficial automation. That's a real risk, not a scare tactic — but say it once, and
let them decide.
